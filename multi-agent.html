Research / Predictive Control, Decentralized Control & Multi-Agent Systems

Predictive Flocking Control for Multi-Agent Systems

Sigma-norm decentralized model predictive control for coordinated motion, lattice formation, and obstacle avoidance in multi-agent systems, evaluated through simulations and comparisons with three established flocking methods.

The research problem

How can a group of robots maintain formation, align their velocities, and avoid obstacles using decentralized model predictive control? This work develops a sigma-norm-based MPC approach in which each agent independently plans its motion using local information.

In practice, each agent uses its own state and information about nearby agents and obstacles within its interaction range. The controller does not require global communication across the entire group or a centralized coordinator.

The study models omnidirectional four-wheeled robots and evaluates the approach numerically. The results shown here are simulations.

Simulation demonstrations

Method

Each agent solves a local MPC optimization problem combining formation spacing, velocity consensus, control smoothness, and control effort. Local interactions account for neighboring agents and obstacles without requiring global communication across the group.

A sigma-norm distance measure is used to formulate the desired spacing between agents and obstacles. Its smooth behavior near zero supports numerically stable optimization and smoother control when agents are close to one another.

The approach is compared with a potential-based flocking method and two MPC-based alternatives. Position irregularity and velocity mismatch provide quantitative measures of group behavior; bootstrap hypothesis tests support the comparative evaluation.
